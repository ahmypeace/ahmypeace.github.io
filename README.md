# Amaka Peace Onebunne

**Technology, Communication & Sociotechnical Systems**

Ph.D. Researcher · Computational Social Science · Human–Machine Communication

I am a Ph.D. student at Temple University's Lew Klein College of Media and Communication. I study the relationships among people, emerging technologies, institutions, and knowledge, with artificial intelligence at the center of much of my current work. 

My research examines human–machine interaction, knowledge systems, algorithmic visibility, information integrity, technological risk and responsibility, and the social and institutional environments developing around increasingly autonomous systems. I use computational, qualitative, and mixed methods across this work.

Questions of culture and geography also run through my research, particularly how technologies and the ideas surrounding them travel across contexts and how Africa and the Global South are positioned within emerging technological futures.

## Current Research


### Human–AI Evaluation

Developing research on synthetic users, persona-based evaluation, and the use of AI systems to approximate human perspectives and judgments.

### Agentic Systems, Failure, and Resilience

Modeling failure propagation across interacting AI agents and studying the system structures that influence cascading failure, recovery, and resilience.

### Human–Machine Knowledge Systems

Studying the production and circulation of machine-generated knowledge, including provenance, authority, recursive information flows, and interactions among people and intelligent systems.

### Journalism, Synthetic Media, and Information Integrity

Studying AI in newsroom practice and conducting cross-national research on how African news media frame deepfakes and synthetic media.

### Algorithms, Representation, and Visibility

Examining algorithmic recommendation, representation, and the feedback processes through which particular ways of seeing Africa become reinforced online.

### Technological Imaginaries and the Global South

Studying narratives of AI expansion, development, investment, and responsibility and the place assigned to the Global South within emerging technological futures.

### Responsible AI and Organizational Accountability

Examining how technology companies define responsible AI, make commitments, assign responsibility, and communicate expectations around safety, evaluation, transparency, and accountability.

## Selected Work

### Journalists' Perception of Artificial Intelligence: The Case of Nigerian Newsrooms

*Digital Journalism*

A study of how Nigerian journalists understand and incorporate AI into newsroom practice, including questions of trust, verification, professional judgment, and cultural context.

### Algorithmic Visibility and the Feedback Loop of Othering: The Case for Africa

*African Journal of Science, Technology, Innovation and Development*

Examines the relationship among algorithmic recommendation, representation, and human engagement and how these feedback processes can reinforce particular representations of Africa online.

### Sociotechnical Imaginaries of AI Expansion: Constructing the Global South as Speculative Market

*4S 2026*

Examines how technological and institutional narratives construct the Global South within imagined futures of AI expansion, development, and investment.

### Does the Brussels Effect Extend from Data Protection to AI Governance in Africa?

*AIES 2026*

A comparative computational study of European and African governance frameworks examining how regulatory influence travels across policy domains and regions.
